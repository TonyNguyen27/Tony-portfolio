## Post 1: Cold email that works (+ psychology)

**Link:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/josh-braun_a-cold-email-that-works-plus-the-underlying-activity-7390452675496054784-uTZy

**Date:** ~Jan 2026 (4mo ago at collection)

**Topic:** Example cold email with psychological breakdown

**Content:**

Example subject: "Lainey Tick James, Half of the cold emails Lainey sends probably end up in spam..."

Body frames deliverability problem for Lainey's team, names ACME/BETA social proof, addresses objections (no fake inboxes, 10-min setup, fair price), CTA: "Worth a test?"

**Why it works (per Josh):** Relevance via teammate name; problem before pitch; preemptive objection handling ("labeling negatives defuses negatives"); low-risk CTA; human short sentences.

**Key Insights:**

- Products have no value without a named problem first
- "Worth a test?" lowers commitment vs. "book a demo"
- Tone should feel like a peer, not a marketer

## Post 2: Psychology of a good cold email (BLUE/GREEN/YELLOW/ORANGE/PINK)

**Link:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/josh-braun_heres-the-psychology-of-a-good-cold-email-activity-7318014648652292096-QDIl

**Date:** ~2024 (1y ago at collection)

**Topic:** Color-coded framework for cold email psychology

**Content:**

Josh breaks cold email into five psychological moves:

- **BLUE** — Why you vs. anyone else? Tie to a specific event/circumstance.
- **GREEN** — What's meaningfully different? Cost of inaction vs. status quo.
- **YELLOW** — "You're not alone" (social proof from peers/neighbors).
- **ORANGE** — Empathy for hesitation to switch; label negatives to defuse them.
- **PINK** — No pressure; gentle ask "like a neighbor over the fence."

**Key Insights:**

- Cold outreach succeeds when prospects feel understood, not sold
- Labeling objections before they voice them reduces defensiveness
- Framework complements Post 1's example email—structure + psychology
